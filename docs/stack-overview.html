Uniswap v3 + Gamma on Hydration

How the concentrated-liquidity stack fits together — the pool, the vault that manages it, the bot that moves it, and the runtime that routes to it.

~30 min read aDOT / HOLLAR · 0.3% chain state checked 2026-09-09

0What we're building

One sentence, then the map.

We are putting a concentrated-liquidity market for aDOT/HOLLAR on Hydration, and automating the part that normally requires a human to babysit it.

Three layers, each solving a problem the one below it creates:

Uniswap v3      deep liquidity from little capital — but someone must
                choose a price range, and move it when the price moves

Gamma           an ERC-20 vault that owns the v3 position, so depositors
                get a fungible token instead of an unmanaged NFT

Keeper bot      decides when and where to move the range, under
                on-chain limits it cannot exceed

Hydration       a Substrate chain with an EVM. The runtime must be told
                the pool exists before native routing can use it

The end state: a user deposits two tokens through the UI, receives a vault token, and the bot keeps their liquidity centred on the market price while the chain's own router sends trade flow through the pool.

1Uniswap v3, enough to explain it

Skip the maths. Six ideas carry the whole design.

1.1 The problem v3 solves

In a classic AMM (Uniswap v2, and Hydration's own Omnipool in spirit), your capital is spread across every possible price, from zero to infinity. If DOT trades between $1.00 and $1.20 all year, the money you posted to quote DOT at $40 — or at 3 cents — does nothing. It sits there providing depth nobody will ever touch.

v3's answer: let the LP pick a price range. Post the same money, but only between $0.99 and $1.20. Inside that range your quote is far deeper than it was; outside it, you quote nothing at all.

The multiplier is large. Relative to a full-range position of the same size:

RangeCapital efficiencyMeaning
±10%~21×our launch band
±5%~41×
±2%~100×
±1%~200×stablecoin territory

The honest version

Concentration multiplies your fees and your losses to arbitrage by the same factor. It is leverage on a market-making position, not free money. A tighter band is a bigger bet that the price stays put.

1.2 Ticks — the price grid

Ranges cannot be arbitrary numbers. v3 quantises price onto a grid:

price = 1.0001 ^ tick

So one tick is one basis point (0.01%). Our pool sits at tick 185,084, which is 1.0907 HOLLAR per aDOT. Every price in this document has a tick behind it, and every log line the keeper prints is in ticks — it is worth getting comfortable with the unit.

Each fee tier also has a tick spacing: range boundaries must land on multiples of it. The 0.3% tier uses spacing 60, so band edges snap to every 60 ticks ≈ 0.6%. This is why the keeper's band width is configured as a multiplier of spacing (BASE_HALF_WIDTH_MULT=16 → 16×60 = 960 ticks ≈ ±10%) rather than as a percentage.

1.3 What your position actually holds

This is the single most useful intuition, and the one people get wrong.

  • Price below your range — you hold only the cheaper asset (aDOT here). The market thinks aDOT is worth less than your lowest quote, so nobody has bought any from you.
  • Price inside — you hold a mix, and it shifts continuously. This is the only state where you earn fees.
  • Price above — you hold only HOLLAR. The market walked up through your whole range and bought every aDOT you had.

Put plainly: you are always selling whatever is going up. That is the market-making trade. You get paid in fees for taking it, and you lose to arbitrageurs when the price trends rather than chops. When price leaves your range entirely you stop earning — which is exactly why somebody has to move the range, and why sections 2 and 3 exist.

1.4 Fees do not compound

Fees from swaps accrue to your position but are not added back into it. They sit as a claimable balance until someone explicitly collects and re-mints them. In vanilla v3 that is a manual chore; in our stack it is the keeper's compound() job (§3.4).

1.5 The oracle built into every pool

Each pool keeps a ring buffer of observations — running sums of the tick over time. Ask observe(3600) and you get the time-weighted average price (TWAP) over the last hour. Because moving a TWAP requires holding a price for a sustained period, not just for one block, it is far more expensive to manipulate than spot. Both Gamma and our keeper lean on it heavily.

The ring starts with one slot, which is not enough. You must call increaseObservationCardinalityNext to grow it — we grow to 2,000, since a 3600s window at Hydration's 2-second blocks needs a floor of 1,801 slots.

A nuance worth knowing

Once the ring is grown, observe(3600) works one hour of wall clock after the pool is initialized, with zero trades ever having happened — the first observation is written at initialize() and the pool extrapolates forward at the current tick. It is bought with the clock, not with trading volume. (Left at cardinality 1, the first trade would overwrite that observation and you really would need an hour of trades.)

1.6 Pools are unique and permanent

The factory maps (token0, token1, fee) to exactly one pool address, forever, via CREATE2. createPool reverts if it already exists. There is no redeploying a pool — if you initialize one at a bad price, you fix the price, you do not fix the pool. The only "fresh start" is a different fee tier, which orphans every address downstream.

Token order also matters and is not up to you: token0 is simply whichever contract address sorts lower. For us that is aDOT 0x0263… = token0, HOLLAR 0x531a… = token1. Every tick sign in the system depends on this.

1.7 What we deployed

The stock Uniswap v3 contract set, unmodified: the core factory, the NonfungiblePositionManager (NFT wrapper for positions), SwapRouter02, QuoterV2 (simulates swaps for pricing), TickLens, Migrator, Staker and Multicall2 — plus the 1bp fee tier. One pool: aDOT/HOLLAR at 0.3%.

Two Hydration-specific quirks

aDOT is a rebasing aToken. Its balance grows with lending interest. v3 never reconciles its internal accounting against balanceOf and has no skim(), so supply interest earned on pool-held aDOT accrues to nobody. It is a small, permanent leak we accepted in exchange for a clean oracle anchor (1 aDOT ≡ 1 DOT, so the pool price is DOT/USD).

aDOT transfers are expensive — about 1.23M gas, because every transfer runs Aave's finalizeTransfer. Any script or UI touching it needs a generous gas limit.

2Why Gamma exists

v3 gives you a position. It does not give you a product.

2.1 The gap

A raw v3 position is an NFT with a fixed range. It has three problems as a product:

  1. It's not fungible. Every LP has a different NFT with different bounds. You cannot build a vault token, farm it, or use it as collateral.
  2. Nobody moves it. Price drifts out of range, the position goes one-sided and stops earning, and it stays that way until the owner notices.
  3. Leftover inventory. Every time you re-centre, you end up holding the wrong ratio of tokens and have to do something with the excess.

Gamma is an active liquidity manager that solves all three. Its core contract, the Hypervisor, is an ERC-20 token that owns v3 positions on your behalf.

2.2 Base and limit positions

The Hypervisor holds two positions, not one:

       limit (narrow, one-sided)
              ┌────┐
              │    │
   ┌──────────┴────┴──────────┐
   │      base (wide, centred on price)      │
   └─────────────────────────────────────────┘
            ▲ current price
  • Base — the wide, symmetric band that does the actual market making. Ours launches at ±10%.
  • Limit — a narrow, one-sided position just off spot, holding whatever inventory was left over after minting the base.

The limit position is the clever part. After a rebalance you are typically long one token. The naive fix is to market-sell the excess — paying a fee and eating slippage. Instead Gamma parks it as a resting one-sided order just outside spot. If the market comes to it, the vault earns the fee instead of paying it. Re-inventorying becomes a revenue line rather than a cost.

2.3 The deposit path

depositor
   │  approve(Hypervisor)          ← not UniProxy. UniProxy forwards
   │                                  from = msg.sender and the
   ▼                                  Hypervisor calls transferFrom
UniProxy.deposit()
   │
   ├──► ClearingV2.clearDeposit()   ← all the gates live here
   │      • vault registered?
   │      • not paused?
   │      • current tick inside the base band?
   │      • spot within 1% of the 1h TWAP?
   │      • both sides non-zero, ratio sane?
   │      • per-tx caps?
   │
   ├──► Hypervisor.deposit()        ← onlyWhitelisted (= UniProxy)
   │      tokens land as IDLE VAULT BALANCE
   │      shares minted, priced at pool spot
   │
   └──► ClearingV2.clearShares()    ← maxTotalSupply, checked after mint

Deposits do not go into the pool

They sit at the vault as idle balance until the keeper sweeps them in. This is deliberate: minting straight into the pool at spot price is manipulable — shove the price, deposit against the distorted composition, shove it back. Gamma only deploys capital during a keeper action, which is itself gated against manipulation. (There is a directDeposit mode. It is off, per spec.)

The gates are why "just add liquidity" is never a one-liner. Two of them bite constantly in practice: the current tick must be inside the base band, and spot must be within 1% of the hour TWAP. A vault left unmanaged between setup and first deposit can drift out of its own band and refuse its own seed.

The empty-vault special case

On the very first deposit (totalSupply() == 0) the ratio check is automatically disabled — getDepositAmount returns (0, deposit1Max), meaning "any proportion, up to the cap". Both sides still have to be non-zero. Worth knowing because a UI that auto-fills the second input from that call will fill it with 0 or with the cap.

The dangerous state is not empty, it is nearly empty: if shares are outstanding but the vault holds zero of one token, getDepositAmount returns (0, 0) and every deposit reverts.

2.4 The guard stack

Gamma splits authority across four contracts so that no single compromised key is fatal.

ContractJobHeld by
HypervisorThe ERC-20 vault. Owns the two v3 positions. Holds no strategy — its bands are whatever the last rebalance set.owner = Admin
UniProxyPublic entry point for deposits. Permissionless — anyone may deposit.governance
ClearingV2Every deposit rule: TWAP guard, ratios, per-tx caps, total supply cap, pause switch.governance
AdminRole split. rebalancers[] and advisors[] are different roles — rebalancing and compounding are separate powers.governance
RebalanceProxyOn-chain policy that binds even a compromised keeper: maxTranslation, maxWidth, minInterval.governance

Our configured limits, per vault:

maxTranslation   500 ticks  (~5%)   one rebalance cannot jump the band further
maxWidth         300 ticks           cannot change band width by more than this
minInterval      21,600s (6h)        hard rate limit between rebalances

The ordering trap this creates

Because a single rebalance may only translate the band 500 ticks, the band must be placed correctly at setup. If the pool price is 980 ticks away from truth when the launch band is set, the keeper's first move exceeds the cap and reverts — and after handover, raising that cap requires a referendum. Fix the price before setting the band, not after.

2.5 Fees

Three separate cuts come out of the same swap fee, and they are easy to confuse:

CutSet whereOurs
Swap fee — what the trader paysfixed at pool creation, immutable0.30%
Protocol fee — v3's cut of thatsetFeeProtocol, factory owner1/4 (4,4)
Gamma fee — vault's cut of what's leftHypervisor fee divisor255 ≈ 0.4%

Note the Gamma fee is a divisor, so bigger number = smaller fee. The code default of 5 means 20%; we launch at 255 to leave nearly everything with LPs. It can never be 0 — that divides by zero and bricks fee harvesting.

3The keeper bot

All the judgement lives off-chain. All the limits live on-chain.

The Hypervisor stores no strategy. Deciding when to move the band and where to put it is the keeper's entire job. It is a small TypeScript service (gamma-hypervisor/keeper) that wakes every block.

3.1 The loop

every block
  │
  ├─ read spot tick
  │
  ├─ TRIGGER?   spot outside the base band
  │             OR drift from band centre > threshold × tickSpacing
  │             (no → maybe compound, else sleep)
  │
  ├─ GATES      every one must pass, all fail CLOSED
  │   1. pool history ≥ MIN_TWAP_WINDOW_SECS
  │   2. |spot − pool TWAP|   ≤ MAX_DEV_TICKS         (100)
  │   3. DIA feed age         ≤ ORACLE_MAX_AGE_SECS
  │   4. |pool − DIA oracle|  ≤ ORACLE_MAX_DEV_TICKS  (200)
  │   5. volatility regime not EXTREME
  │   6. dwell / min-interval satisfied
  │   7. keeper gas above floor
  │
  ├─ PLACE      new band centred on the TWAP tick, not spot
  │
  ├─ PREFLIGHT  callStatic the exact tx; a revert here costs nothing
  │
  └─ SUBMIT     keeper → RebalanceProxy → Admin → Hypervisor.rebalance()

3.2 Trigger versus placement

A deliberate split, and a good one to understand:

  • Spot decides whether to act. It is responsive — you want to notice quickly that price left the band.
  • The TWAP decides where the new band goes. It is slow and expensive to manipulate.

So an attacker can push spot and make the keeper wake up, but cannot drag the band to the price they pushed. The worst they achieve is a wasted transaction.

3.3 The external-truth clamp

Gate 4 is the important one. The pool's own TWAP can be walked over time by someone willing to hold a price — but they cannot move the exchanges that DIA aggregates. Requiring the pool to agree with the external feed within 200 ticks (~2%) makes "manipulate the pool, wait for the keeper" unprofitable.

Everything fails closed. An unreadable feed, a stale feed, a TWAP that won't compute — all of them mean skip, never proceed anyway. The failure mode is an idle keeper, which is recoverable; the alternative is placing real money at a manipulated price, which is not.

3.4 Compounding

Separate job, separate clock, separate role (advisors[], not rebalancers[]). compound() harvests accrued swap fees and re-mints them into the existing ticks — and because it re-mints the vault's whole idle balance, it is also what sweeps in new deposits.

It runs on a timer (COMPOUND_INTERVAL_SECS, default 300s) but passes the same price gates. Short interval is intentional: an attack on a sweep scales with the size of the pile being tipped in, so frequent small sweeps stay below the threshold where attacking one beats the swap fee.

Check this flag

COMPOUND_ENABLED defaults to false. With it off there is no periodic sweep at all, and a deposit sits as idle vault balance until a drift-triggered rebalance happens to fire — which, if price is sitting mid-band, could be a long time.

3.5 What the keeper cannot do

By design, and worth stating because people assume otherwise:

  • It cannot add liquidity. It only moves and compounds what depositors have already put in. An empty vault gives it nothing to do.
  • It cannot exceed the proxy caps — translation, width, interval are enforced in Solidity.
  • It cannot pull liquidity in an emergency. That is Admin.pullLiquidity, a governance path, not a keeper path.
  • It cannot fix a stale pool price. Only trading does that, and trading needs liquidity.

4The Substrate side

Hydration is not an EVM chain with a Substrate bolt-on. It is the other way round.

4.1 Two worlds, one chain

Hydration is a Substrate parachain whose native pallets (Omnipool, Stableswap, lending) are Rust runtime code. It also runs an EVM via pallet-frontier, which is where Uniswap v3 and Gamma live. The interesting engineering is at the seam.

Assets have numeric IDs on the Substrate side (aDOT = 1001, HOLLAR = 222) and contract addresses on the EVM side. For Erc20-kind assets the runtime resolves an ID to its registered contract address, not to the synthetic 0x…01++id alias.

This bit has bitten us

A pool built from alias addresses is invisible to the runtime's find_pool — and for aDOT the alias reverts on transfer, so it could not be funded either. Anything ordering-dependent must use registry contract addresses.

4.2 How the runtime routes through an EVM pool

UniswapV3TradeExecutor (runtime/hydradx/src/evm/uniswap_v3_trade_executor.rs) implements the same TradeExecution trait that Omnipool and Stableswap implement. That is what lets pallet-route-executor treat a v3 pool as just another venue in a multi-hop route.

It works by having Rust call Solidity:

quoting     QuoterV2.quoteExactInputSingle / quoteExactOutputSingle
discovery   Factory.getPool(token0, token1, fee)
state       Pool.slot0(), Pool.liquidity()
execution   SwapRouter02.exactInputSingle / exactOutputSingle
plumbing    ERC20.approve, ERC20.balanceOf

After every executed swap it reports the trade to the EMA oracle under UNISWAPV3_SOURCE — which is what lets DCA price legs that route through the v3 pool.

4.3 Why the pool must be registered

The executor reads its three addresses from pallet-parameters at call time:

parameters.uniswapV3Factory
parameters.uniswapV3SwapRouter
parameters.uniswapV3Quoter

Unset, every route through v3 errors with "uniswapv3: quoter not configured". They are written by parameters.setUniswapV3Addresses(factory, swapRouter, quoter), which is ensure_root — no configurable origin, so it is a Root-track referendum, full stop.

Until that lands, the pool works fine for anyone calling the EVM directly, and is invisible to the app, the SDK router and DCA.

4.4 Governance identities

Contract calls that need privileged origins go through pallet-dispatcher, which dispatches an EVM call as a specific on-chain identity:

Dispatcher callActs asNarrowest track
dispatchAsTreasury0x6d6f646c70792f7472737279…5 — Treasurer
dispatchAsAaveManager0xaa7e…aa7e09 — EconomicParameters
dispatchAsEmergencyAdmin0xaa7e…aa7e1TC motion

These are not interchangeable. Our v3 factory is owned by 0xaa7e…aa7e0, so setFeeProtocol must be dispatched as the Aave manager — dispatched as anything else it reverts.

Governance calls lie about success

dispatchAsX returns Ok at the outer level even when the inner EVM call reverted, and utility.BatchCompleted fires regardless. That combination silently bricked money-market referendum 322. Never treat a green extrinsic as evidence — read the resulting contract state.

5SDK and UI

Two consumers of the same pool, with different needs.

5.1 SDK — sdk-next

The trading SDK gained a full uniswapv3 AMM module (packages/sdk-next/src/pool/amm/uniswapv3/) so the smart order router can quote v3 alongside Omnipool, Stableswap and XYK. It:

  • discovers the pool by reading the factory from chain state — no hardcoded addresses;
  • loads the tick bitmap around the current price (a window of ~20,000 ticks each side, capped at 12 bitmap words per side so one tight-spacing pool cannot flood the RPC);
  • reimplements v3 swap maths in TypeScript so it can quote locally rather than paying an RPC round trip per candidate route;
  • reports when the tick window was cut short, because past the loaded ticks the walk hits zero-liquidity sentinels and would over-quote.

5.2 UI

The vault appears on the liquidity page. It needs to handle states that no existing Gamma deployment has: an empty vault and an empty pool. Concretely —

  • getDepositAmount returns (0, cap) on an empty vault, so auto-filling the paired amount from it produces 0 or the cap. Needs a totalSupply() == 0 branch that prices the pair off the pool instead.
  • The same call reverts on a zero input — guard before calling on every keystroke.
  • getTotalAmounts() returns (0,0) and totalSupply() is 0, so TVL, share price and APR all divide by zero.
  • A band of 0 / 0 renders as "Not started" — that is not a bug, it means the handover has not run.
  • Approval goes to the Hypervisor, not the UniProxy.

6Deployment steps

Two repos, two referenda, one strict order.

6.1 Phase 1 — the venue (uniswap-v3-deploy/mainnet)

StepDoes
00-preflight.jsRead-only gate: chain, key, gas, asset resolution, ordering.
02-deploy.js15 steps of stock Uniswap bytecode — factory, NFT descriptors, position manager, SwapRouter02, QuoterV2, TickLens, Migrator, Staker, Multicall2, 1bp tier. Resumable.
03-create-pool.jsCreates the pool, initializes it at the oracle price, and grows the observation ring to 2,000 in chunks. Adds no liquidity.

Referendum A (Root), before deploying: list the deploy key on EVMAccounts::ContractDeployer. Since runtime 443, contract creation is permissioned; an unlisted key's CREATE fails and produces no EVM receipt, so a naive script hangs rather than erroring.

Referendum B (Root), after deploying: setFeeProtocol(4,4) on the pool plus parameters.setUniswapV3Addresses. Cannot be built before the contracts exist, because it carries their addresses.

6.2 Phase 2 — the vault (gamma-hypervisor/mainnet)

StepDoes
00-preflight.jsProves the configured factory really maps the pair to the configured pool; checks roles and guards.
02-deploy.jsDeploys the Hypervisor (via HypervisorFactory), ClearingV2, UniProxy, Admin, RebalanceProxy. Wires keeper roles and caps. Leaves ownership with the deploy key — bootstrap posture.

6.3 The order that matters

1  Referendum A — whitelist the deploy key
2  Deploy v3, create + initialize the pool at the oracle price
3  Grow the observation ring, wait 1h of wall clock
4  Deploy Gamma (bootstrap posture)
5  RE-ANCHOR the pool price if it has drifted        ← before step 6
6  03-handover.js — band, whitelist, ownership
7  Start the keeper, confirm it can act
8  First deposit through UniProxy
9  Referendum B — protocol fee + router registration  ← parallel to 5-8

Step 5 before step 6 is not a preference. The handover sets the band at whatever tick the pool reads at that moment, and the proxy's 500-tick translation cap means a badly-placed band cannot be walked back without governance.

Operational rules that keep biting

Always pass ENV_FILE. Unset, it falls back to mainnet/.env, and the chain ID (222222) is identical on mainnet and on our forks — the guard cannot save you.

Addresses are never portable. They are nonce-derived. Nothing downstream may carry an address from a rehearsal or a fork.

Gas is pinned, not estimated. Hydration's eth_estimateGas under-reports; an underpriced transaction is dropped at apply without a receipt, and the script hangs on .wait() forever.
